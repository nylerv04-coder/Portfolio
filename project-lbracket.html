// CASE FILE: L-BRACKET STRUCTURAL STUDY

TYPE: Independent Study
DATE: November 2024
TOOLS: SolidWorks, Ansys, FDM 3D Printer
MATERIAL: PLA

// OBJECTIVE

This project served as a hands-on introduction to the full engineering design loop — from CAD modeling and FEA simulation through to physical fabrication and load testing. The goal was simple: design two versions of an L-bracket, one structurally naive and one optimized, then verify the performance difference empirically with a 3D printer and dead-weight testing.

// DESIGN APPROACH

Both brackets were modeled in SolidWorks. The first — the baseline design — featured a sharp 90° internal corner at the bend. Sharp interior corners are a well-known stress concentration site; under load, the notch effect focuses tensile stress at the tip of the corner, dramatically reducing the force required for crack initiation and propagation.

The optimized bracket addressed this through two modifications: the sharp interior corner was replaced with a generous fillet radius to redistribute stress over a larger area, and a triangular gusset was added to the inner face of the bend to stiffen the joint and shorten the moment arm acting on the critical cross-section. Print orientation was also varied between iterations to align layer boundaries away from the primary tensile load path, taking advantage of PLA's anisotropic properties.

// SIMULATION — ANSYS FEA

Prior to printing, both designs were run through a static structural analysis in Ansys. A fixed boundary condition was applied to the mounting face and a vertical downward load was applied to the horizontal shelf. The von Mises stress contour plots confirmed the hypothesis: the baseline design showed a pronounced stress concentration at the sharp corner exceeding material yield, while the optimized design distributed load smoothly through the fillet and into the gusset, keeping peak stresses well within the safe range.

// PHYSICAL TESTING

After printing both brackets in PLA, each was clamped to a fixed surface and loaded incrementally with dead weight. Results were consistent with simulation predictions.

Baseline Sharp 90° corner, no gusset < 10 kg Brittle fracture at inner corner
Optimized Fillet + triangular gusset > 10 kg (no failure) —
[ BASELINE — FAILED ] Fractured cleanly at the inner corner under 10 kg of applied load. Failure was sudden with no visible prior deformation — consistent with stress concentration-driven brittle fracture in FDM PLA.
[ OPTIMIZED — SURVIVED ] Held full 10 kg test load with no fracture or visible deformation. The fillet and gusset successfully redistributed stress away from the critical joint region, validating both the simulation and the design logic.

// TAKEAWAYS

This project demonstrated the real-world impact of stress concentration geometry on part performance — a <5-minute CAD change (adding a fillet and gusset) more than doubled effective load capacity. It also reinforced the importance of print orientation as a design variable in FDM parts, and gave practical experience closing the loop between simulation outputs and physical test data.
