PROJECT OBJECTIVE

The VEX Pushback competition challenges teams to design robots capable of collecting and scoring irregular octagonal objects — called octoballs — at three different height levels. Our solution addressed this with two integrated systems: a multi-height roller intake and scoring mechanism, and a custom omnidirectional differential swerve drivetrain built for precise, agile maneuvering on the competition field.

SCORING SYSTEM

Multiple intake and scoring concepts were evaluated using a Value Opportunity Analysis weighing reliability, speed, cost, and maneuverability. A chain-driven three-sprocket roller intake with rubber band compliant rollers was selected for its ability to handle the irregular octoball shape reliably. A pneumatically actuated intake bar assists with initial pickup. Objects are guided into a storage column and either released for low-goal scoring or transferred to an outtake mechanism for medium and high goals — eliminating the need to reposition the robot between scoring heights.

SWERVE DRIVETRAIN

The drivetrain uses four independent differential swerve modules, each providing full translational, rotational, and lateral movement by independently controlling the torque and steering of each wheel. This omnidirectional capability enables the fast directional changes and precise path following required in competition. Traditional tank and H-drive configurations were evaluated against criteria including mass, manufacturability, cost, and reliability before selecting the differential swerve approach.

Each module uses a dual-purpose gear system where two VEX V5 Smart Motors with 6:1 gear cartridges cooperatively control both wheel orientation (steering) and wheel speed (driving). A 24-tooth driving bevel gear meshes with a 48-tooth bevel gear on a 2.75-inch wheel, which is surrounded by rollers for low-friction pivoting. Modules are primarily constructed from PETG and PETG-Carbon Fiber and are fully modular — each can be assembled, serviced, or replaced without affecting the rest of the drivetrain. Theoretical maximum speed is approximately 81.05 inches per second, and the full assembly fits within the required 15 cubic inch envelope.

SIMULATION & ANALYSIS

Ansys Explicit Dynamics was used to simulate the impact physics of the octoball entering the scoring tube at varying velocities, modeling material properties and the coefficient of restitution to determine the maximum safe intake speed. Torque-speed analysis of the VEX V5 motors confirmed the selected gear ratios meet target performance goals for both the intake and drivetrain systems.

RESULTS

Prototype testing validated successful intake, controlled transport, and multi-height scoring. The compliant roller system handles the irregular octoball shape reliably and the pneumatic intake bar improves pickup consistency. Structural modifications reduced deflection and improved rigidity while keeping the design compact. Preliminary swerve module testing confirmed proper gear mesh and smooth motor-driven rotation under load. Simulation data established safe intake velocity thresholds, ensuring maximum rapid-fire capability with a high retention rate at each goal height.
